# Entity context review

This guide reviews selected entity relationships in the forty-video sample. Codex checked the cited notes and source passages; the [literal name index](index.md) does not independently resolve identity, endorsement, ownership, or a financial track record.

**Somewhere.com is a disclosed commercial interest.** The participant describes involvement in the recruiting business and promotes international hiring. Salary and overhead comparisons remain interested, self-reported claims. See [staffing and delegation](../sources/elvH4as1Qzs.md) and [the recruiting discussion](../sources/PTlE4pRylcs.md).

**Storage Squad and Bolt Storage occupy different parts of the operating story.** The sample distinguishes a student pickup-and-delivery operation from real-estate ownership. A transition narrative describes divided partner responsibilities, refinancing, and a later sale. Do not treat all references to storage as one unchanged business model. See [the transition and sale account](../sources/RoTui9BCW4k.md).

**Famous technology founders are often counterexamples.** Steve Jobs, Elon Musk, and Mark Zuckerberg appear in a critique of copying mature or exceptional technology-founder stories when starting a small service business. A mention is not necessarily a recommendation or a claim that those businesses lack merit. See [early business learning](../sources/H1GTRcbuSGg.md) and [practical entrepreneurial skills](../sources/7_wq5V7-cB4.md).

**Health authorities and products require a different evidence standard.** The fasting discussion invokes medical expertise, but transcript accuracy does not establish clinical validity. See [the health-claim review](../sources/3QAV1Avmg9Y.md).

**Partner and advisor stories remain attributed accounts.** A wealth manager's free consultation illustrates one qualified-prospect sales approach, with an explicit warning against applying it everywhere. See [the advisor story](../sources/GamOfEftPUA.md).
