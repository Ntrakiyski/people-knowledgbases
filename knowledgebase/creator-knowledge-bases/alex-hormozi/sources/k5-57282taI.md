# How The Top 1% Actually Think About Trust

This page describes the selected source's claims and advice. The summary and interpretations are machine-extracted drafts; quotations have been matched against the exported transcript.

Hormozi proposes a behavioral account of trust based on who is exposed to harm and whether harm comes from another person or consequences of failed action or advice. He recommends looking at a person's record and incentives before disclosing something material. The relational benefit is greater shared context, which can enable better help. Trustworthiness then requires both a safe immediate response and not weaponizing the disclosure later. He separately emphasizes keeping commitments and giving reliable advice. The model is his conceptual framework, illustrated with hypothetical family examples and a planned company disclosure, not a validated psychology taxonomy.

## Insights

### Trust exposes something valued to possible harm

The model treats disclosure, delegated responsibility, and following advice as different ways of becoming vulnerable. It should not be mistaken for an exhaustive definition of human trust.

> if I trust someone it means that I make myself punishable by them

## Context and limits

The four-type model and the claim that one betrayal can end trust are the speaker's generalizations. His wife/mother example is explicitly made up. The company presentation is planned for the next day, not a completed trust-building outcome. Strategic vulnerability does not establish that all disclosure is prudent.
