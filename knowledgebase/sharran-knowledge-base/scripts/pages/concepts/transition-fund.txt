Sharran's transition fund is a reserve intended to buy time when active income stops or needs to change. Its target is expressed in months of actual expenses, not a single small emergency-dollar amount.

He proposes six to twelve months of spending. His example assumes $4,000 in monthly costs and builds $24,000 by saving $2,000 each month for a year. That accumulated balance covers six months at the stated spending rate. The reserve does not replace the former salary dollar for dollar; it supports the expenses that salary had been paying.

The purpose extends beyond surviving an unexpected bill. He describes time to find another job, seek a better opportunity, or retrain and reinvent a career. A person with a buffer has more room to make a considered transition instead of accepting the first available option under immediate cash pressure.

Within his milestone sequence, the fund protects the active income foundation before surplus is directed more aggressively toward long-term assets. It is a defensive form of optionality rather than the final financial-freedom goal.

His proposed storage choices require care in interpreting the teaching. He mentions both high-yield savings and stocks, emphasizing growth and daily access. The video does not analyze stock-price volatility or the possibility that the balance falls when the reserve is needed. Daily tradability and stable reserve value are different properties.

The page therefore preserves his sizing and purpose without presenting the stock suggestion as proven capital protection. The essential measure is how long the reserve can support the current spending requirement during an income transition.
