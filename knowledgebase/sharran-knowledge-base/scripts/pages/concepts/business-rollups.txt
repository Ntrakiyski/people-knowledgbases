A roll-up acquires several businesses and integrates them into a larger operating platform. In Sharran’s account, value can grow through both the combined earnings and the multiple a later buyer is willing to pay. Merely owning several separate companies does not accomplish the same thing.

His central example is a property-management platform assembled from eleven businesses. Sellers received a combination of cash and equity, allowing some participation in the eventual platform sale. He reports buying at roughly three to four times EBITDA and selling at 6.6 times. Those figures describe his claimed transaction experience; they are not a rule that acquiring enough small companies automatically produces a higher valuation.

Operating integration is the mechanism between acquisition and a credible platform. His dental-practice illustration combines supply purchasing, scheduling and billing procedures, and finance functions. Shared systems should make the earnings base more predictable. He also calls for revenue growth inside existing locations, rather than making every improvement depend on buying another office. Geographic spread can help offset uneven performance at individual sites.

A buyer’s alternative explains why the platform might command a premium. Acquiring eleven established businesses can save more time than building the same customer base, workforce, locations, and processes from scratch. Sharran calls acquisition conversations buy-versus-build conversations: a buyer weighs the price of an operating organization against the execution risk and delay of constructing it.

The resulting thesis has two parts to examine separately. The platform needs actual, durable earnings, and the valuation assumption needs a buyer who values its scale and capabilities. His improvised comparisons of small and large firms emphasize this possibility without providing an industry-independent formula. Integration quality and operating evidence are therefore central to the story, rather than optional work after financial engineering has already created the return.
