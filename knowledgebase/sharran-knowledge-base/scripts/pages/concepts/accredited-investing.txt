Srivatsaa makes accredited investing the equity rich milestone in his MONEY framework. He connects having $1 million of net worth excluding a primary residence with United States accredited investor eligibility and describes it as a change in the investment opportunities available to a household.

The opportunities he emphasizes are private equity, hedge funds, and startup deals. Before this stage, he says his own investments were entirely in public markets; afterward, he describes a portfolio dominated by private opportunities. He therefore presents eligibility as an access milestone, distinct from simply holding a larger balance in the same retail account.

His proposed route to the threshold combines higher earning skills, restrained lifestyle spending, and public market investing. Reaching the milestone is still not his definition of financial freedom. In the same framework, freedom requires passive income to cover expenses, so private market access is presented as an intermediate tool rather than the endpoint.

The video makes strong claims about superior private returns, including a 54% comparison, without supplying supporting evidence in the transcript. It also simplifies eligibility into one wealth threshold rather than explaining a complete set of legal categories and offering conditions. The source supports describing Srivatsaa’s access thesis, not treating accreditation as a certification of investor skill, investment quality, or safety. A private deal remains a separate allocation decision with its own terms and downside.
