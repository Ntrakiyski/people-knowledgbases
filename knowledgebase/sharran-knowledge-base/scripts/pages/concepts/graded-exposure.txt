Srivatsaa proposes a graded exposure ladder for the fear of being judged or found inadequate. He argues that this fear often sounds like preparation: someone says more time or knowledge is needed when the underlying concern is being seen before feeling fully capable. He does not claim that every delay has this cause, but asks whether waiting is actually supplying useful preparation.

## Increase the audience in manageable steps

His content creation example has three stages. First, write the post or script and send it to one trusted person for a friendly reaction, rather than permission to exist. Second, share it in a smaller setting, such as a private community, newsletter, or close friends channel. Third, publish it on the main platform.

Each step supplies experience with judgment before the next increase in stakes. The work may improve through feedback, and the person has already survived two rounds of sharing by the time the widest audience sees it. The procedure makes action the source of new information instead of requiring complete confidence in advance.

## Repetition changes familiarity with the task

Srivatsaa connects the principle to his account of completing thirty nine interviews before joining Goldman Sachs. He says he repeatedly feared being exposed as someone without the expected background, but became more comfortable through the repetitions. He also uses an Oprah career narrative to illustrate facing public vulnerability rather than hiding it. These are his accounts, not independent evidence of a universal psychological effect.

The practical distinction is between a leap into maximum stakes and a sequence of deliberate encounters. Srivatsaa contrasts this with simply telling oneself to be brave or waiting for fear to disappear. In the video, the ladder is a communication and performance tool; it is not presented as a clinical protocol or a promise to eliminate every form of anxiety.
