SCHO is one of the four funds Sharran names in his example of building a small income-producing portfolio. He later describes dividing the allocation equally among the holdings.

The source uses the combined portfolio to teach a contribution and ownership habit. It does not separately analyze SCHO’s duration, expenses, distribution behavior, or possible price changes. Its inclusion records what the speaker says he holds; it is not evidence that the portfolio functions as a guaranteed cash balance or that the allocation fits every investor. The broader lesson concerns starting with a concrete, observable investment process.
