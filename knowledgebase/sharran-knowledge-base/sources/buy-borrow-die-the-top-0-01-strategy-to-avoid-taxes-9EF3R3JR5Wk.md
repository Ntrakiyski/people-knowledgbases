# Buy, Borrow, Die: The Top 0.01% Strategy to Avoid Taxes

Sharran describes a buy-borrow-die strategy he calls the legacy loop. He first distinguishes assets, liabilities, and collateral, then focuses on assets lenders can value reliably. He presents home-equity lines, securities-backed credit, and permanent-life-insurance loans as ways to obtain liquidity without selling the underlying holding, and argues this can preserve compounding and avoid a sale-triggered capital-gains event. His tax and legal descriptions are claims about the US setting, not independently verified rules or universal outcomes.

He uses his son’s land investment to illustrate pooling policy loans and partner capital, and explains his own preference for intentionally structured whole-life policies for his children. Crucially, he discusses rising HELOC payments and foreclosure, falling securities values and forced liquidation, and insurance-policy lapse when loans outgrow cash value. The final section describes stepped-up basis and heirs selling a slice of a portfolio to settle outstanding borrowing, making clear that the debt still has to be repaid.

## Concepts covered

- [Asset-backed borrowing](../concepts/asset-backed-borrowing.md): Using valuable holdings as collateral to access liquidity without selling them.
- [Buy, borrow, die](../concepts/buy-borrow-die.md): A strategy of retaining assets, borrowing for liquidity, and settling debt during inheritance.
- [Risk Management](../concepts/risk-management.md): Evaluate downside, obligations, behavioral errors, and execution dependence before and after committing resources.
- [Whole-life policy borrowing](../concepts/whole-life-banking.md): Using a permanent insurance policy’s accumulated cash value as collateral for loans.
