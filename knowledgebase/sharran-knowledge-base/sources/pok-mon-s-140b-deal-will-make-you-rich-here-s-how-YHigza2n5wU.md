# Pokémon’s $140B Deal Will Make You Rich (Here’s How)

Sharran presents Pokémon’s early development as a financing and ownership case study. In his account, milestone funding from Nintendo and outside contract work failed to cover Game Freak’s prolonged development costs; emergency financing from Creatures helped finish the game. His Deal DNA analysis separates capital, creative work, intellectual-property ownership, distribution, and future upside. His figures and historical details are his account, with some funding amounts explicitly described as undisclosed.

He distinguishes check equity from sweat equity and examines each participant’s risks, including family support outside the formal transaction. The broader lesson is a proof stack of demonstrated delivery, relationships built before crisis, and visible commitment. Founders can show prototypes, users, revenue, and case studies; job seekers can solve a company problem before being hired. His 10–10–forever rule calls for deeply supporting ten people over ten years.

## Concepts covered

- [Capital stack](../concepts/capital-stack.md): The capital stack maps the financing and ownership contributions supporting a deal.
- [Deal structure](../concepts/deal-structure.md): A transaction’s terms distribute control, ownership, obligations, and future upside.
- [Intellectual property ownership](../concepts/intellectual-property-ownership.md): Reusable creative rights can participate in many future revenue streams.
- [Proof Stack](../concepts/proof-stack.md): Demonstrate delivery, trust, and commitment before asking others to back a larger promise.
- [Relationship Capital](../concepts/relationship-capital.md): Build durable support through attention, useful contributions, and trust established before it is needed.
- [Sweat equity](../concepts/sweat-equity.md): Ownership can be earned by contributing work and capability rather than only cash.
