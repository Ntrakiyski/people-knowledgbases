# I’ll Delete Your Financial Anxiety, Just Give Me 18 Mins

Sharran interprets failure through three fears. Fear of exposure appears as endless preparation or waiting to feel ready; he proposes gradually increasing exposure and deciding a date for a hard decision. Fear of loss often protects status or identity, so he recommends asking whether one would choose the same asset or path today, writing exit criteria, and specifying what would make a project successful. His personal and company stories illustrate these ideas rather than establish their universal causes.

Fear of error leads people to defend bad choices instead of correcting them. He recommends an after-action review comparing plans with results, then requiring expensive mistakes to produce a reusable principle or checklist. His account of losing a million dollars to a fraudulent investment leads to the four goods: people, intentions, rationale, and contracts. Finally, a memorable image or story can become a quick trigger for applying a learned rule when temptation returns.

## Concepts covered

- [After-action review](../concepts/after-action-review.md): Comparing intended and actual results to identify a gap and change the next attempt.
- [Decision Frameworks](../concepts/decision-framework.md): Define the context, evidence, deadline, and exit conditions for consequential choices.
- [Graded exposure](../concepts/graded-exposure.md): Building tolerance for judgment by moving from low-stakes practice to broader exposure.
