# Once I Learned This at Goldman Sachs, My Life Changed

Sharran recounts joining Goldman Sachs after business school and later reconsidering the economics and purpose of advisory work. He criticizes recurring fees, illustrates their compounding effect, and explains why he preferred structured notes that he believed gave clients more predictable outcomes. His product descriptions are simplified personal examples. A client’s business sale then made the contrast between transaction fees and ownership proceeds concrete for him.

He describes an investor-client introducing him to a struggling real-estate company, discovering alleged embezzlement during diligence, and backing his move into operating ownership. He says he extracted home equity through a private arrangement to buy his stake, received mentorship, and took no salary for an extended period while learning the job. The account includes his claimed growth and exit of Telus Properties. He closes by separating personal vocation from status: employment, professional services, and entrepreneurship can all be valid paths, but becoming excellent at work one hates is a poor goal.

## Concepts covered

- [Career fit](../concepts/career-fit.md): Choose work that matches personal fit rather than professional status alone.
- [Equity Ownership](../concepts/equity-ownership.md): Participate in business value through ownership, with the corresponding capital and execution risks.
- [Investment fee drag](../concepts/investment-fee-drag.md): Recurring investment fees reduce both current returns and the future growth of capital.
- [Risk Management](../concepts/risk-management.md): Evaluate downside, obligations, behavioral errors, and execution dependence before and after committing resources.
