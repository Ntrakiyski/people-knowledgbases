# The Most Underrated Skill That Could Make You Rich

Srivatsaa argues that communication can influence career outcomes and teaches three techniques drawn from his own speaking practice. First, the one, two, three, too many rule limits an explanation to three main ideas, as in reducing, deferring, or eliminating taxes. Second, he recommends using simple geometric shapes to turn a complex web of ideas into a visual framework that listeners can remember and repeat. He credits speaking coach Richard Green, as rendered in the transcript, with helping him learn this packaging technique.

The third technique is the process transcribed as format: why, what, how, and now. Why establishes relevance, urgency, and credibility; what provides an overview; how explains implementation; now identifies the first actionable step. He applies this sequence to presentations and everyday meeting updates, warning against jumping into procedural detail before listeners have context. As a practical aid, he suggests speaking a rough idea into AI and requesting that structure explicitly. Autobiographical claims about coaching cost and the general superiority of communication to technical skill remain the speaker’s framing, rather than demonstrated universal career rules.
