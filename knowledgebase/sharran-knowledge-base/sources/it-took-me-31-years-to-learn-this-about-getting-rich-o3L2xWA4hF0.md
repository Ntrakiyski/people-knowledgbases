# It Took Me 31 Years to Learn This About Getting Rich

Srivatsaa defines rich as high income, wealthy as high net worth, and financially free as having passive income cover expenses. The first stage requires developing earning skills and retaining a surplus: an $800,000 income fully consumed produces less investable capacity than a smaller income with substantial savings. He recounts keeping household spending at an earlier level while his own business income and net worth increased.

For the wealth stage he emphasizes accumulating income producing assets, time, and returns, and describes business equity and real estate as two golden stairways. Public ETFs are offered as a way to gain exposure before directly owning a company or property. Some categorical claims, including that stocks cannot be financed with borrowing, are his statements rather than verified facts. Finally he explains passive income as income prefunded with effort or capital, using royalties, affiliate income, revenue sharing, dividend portfolios, and real estate as illustrations. His revenue share example still requires network maintenance, showing that passive need not mean no continuing work. The closing goal is freedom to choose one’s day and share the outcome with loved ones.

## Concepts covered

- [Compound Growth](../concepts/compound-growth.md): Returns can earn further returns as an invested balance grows over time.
- [Equity Ownership](../concepts/equity-ownership.md): Participate in business value through ownership, with the corresponding capital and execution risks.
- [Financial Freedom](../concepts/financial-freedom.md): Financial freedom means recurring passive income covers spending and makes work optional.
- [Income Margin](../concepts/income-margin.md): The surplus between earnings and spending that funds future assets and financial resilience.
- [Passive Income](../concepts/passive-income.md): Income prefunded with money or effort can reduce dependence on ongoing paid work.
