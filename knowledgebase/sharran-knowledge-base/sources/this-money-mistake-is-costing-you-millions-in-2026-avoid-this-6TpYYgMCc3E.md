# This Money Mistake Is Costing You Millions in 2026 (Avoid THIS)

Sharran argues that earning more and trimming small purchases can miss four larger drains: inflation, taxes, interruption, and fees. He describes idle cash losing purchasing power and proposes comparing cash accounts and inflation-linked securities. His discussion of TIPS and the TIP ETF is simplified, and his suggestion that these or high-yield savings necessarily preserve purchasing power should remain attributed to him. He next advocates deliberate tax planning, including account location, holding periods, and attention to legal incentives rather than treating tax filing as a complete strategy.

He distinguishes a tax preparer’s compliance role from a strategist’s forward planning and suggests having the strategist coordinate with the preparer. Interruption refers to withdrawing or trading from a long-term portfolio because of fear, headlines, or consumption, thereby disrupting compounding. The final section illustrates recurring fee drag, compares QQQ with QQQM using the speaker’s claimed fee figures, and offers a practical adviser question: explain how everyone gets paid. His numerical return gaps, tax percentages, and ETF comparisons are examples or assertions in the video, not independently verified current facts.

## Concepts covered

- [Compound Growth](../concepts/compound-growth.md): Returns can earn further returns as an invested balance grows over time.
- [Inflation protection](../concepts/inflation-protection.md): Evaluate investment results in purchasing power as well as nominal dollars.
- [Investment fee drag](../concepts/investment-fee-drag.md): Recurring investment fees reduce both current returns and the future growth of capital.
- [Tax Efficiency](../concepts/tax-efficiency.md): Assess how account placement, timing, and applicable rules affect after-tax investment outcomes.
