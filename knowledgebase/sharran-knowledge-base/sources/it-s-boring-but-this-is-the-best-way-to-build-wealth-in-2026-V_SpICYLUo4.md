# It's Boring, But This Is The BEST Way to Build Wealth in 2026

Srivatsaa advocates rolling up local service businesses: acquire several companies in the same sector, combine their operations, and eventually sell the platform at a higher earnings multiple. He recounts buying eleven property management companies with a mix of borrowing and seller equity, paying roughly three to four times EBITDA and exiting at 6.6 times EBITDA for a claimed $198 million over three and a half years. His descriptions of SBA lending and no money out of pocket are presented as his account, not verified financing rules or a universally available structure.

He stresses that acquisition alone does not create an effective platform. Buyers must understand acquisition and operating costs, claim priority if the deal fails, and the assets actually transferred. Integration means shared scheduling, billing, supplies, and finance, followed by stable earnings and organic growth. His three closing lessons are to understand a buyer’s buy versus build calculation, recognize how diversified operating scale can absorb local setbacks, and distinguish the valuation of a single operating business from a larger platform. The higher multiples and resilience he describes are potential outcomes that depend on the business, integration, and deal terms.
