# Conviction and Investment Rationale

> What is the high conviction bet that you can make?

[This Warren Buffett Rule Can Make You Rich (Copy THIS)](../sources/this-warren-buffett-rule-can-make-you-rich-copy-this-tzUvZijbpjY.md), 0:02:07.

Conviction in Sharran’s framework means having an intelligible ownership thesis, rather than feeling enthusiastic about a popular asset. The investor should understand what is owned, why it is attractive, and which future evidence would undermine the decision. That understanding can support action during fearful conditions, but confidence itself does not eliminate downside.

His practical version is a written rationale answering four questions: why own this, what should happen, how long will it be held, and when would selling be justified? Expected outcomes might concern revenue, a market’s expansion, or income distributions. Exit conditions should relate to a changed thesis rather than merely the discomfort of seeing a lower price. He offers a waiting rule after a market decline as one personal example, supported by his claimed historical analysis; it is not an established guarantee of recovery.

A second filter is the investment punch card he attributes to Buffett. Imagining only ten investment decisions across a lifetime forces an opportunity to compete for scarce attention and capital. Sharran uses the exercise to resist fear of missing out and reject deals that are merely available. The limit is a thought experiment, not a literal requirement to own ten assets.

The same discipline can lead to declining an investment. He recommends supporting a friend as a customer instead of buying equity in an unfamiliar startup simply from social pressure. His admission of limited Bitcoin understanding similarly separates curiosity from a justified large commitment.

Conviction therefore works with quality assessment and position sizing. It should make the reasons for exposure clearer, including the limits of one’s knowledge, rather than supply a story for ignoring losses or concentrating an entire financial base in one bet.
