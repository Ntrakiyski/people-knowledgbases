# Persistence

> I just won't quit today.

[It Took Me 30 Years To Learn What I’ll Tell You In 19 Minutes](../sources/it-took-me-30-years-to-learn-what-i-ll-tell-you-in-19-minutes-yU1YFrzeHSc.md), 0:01:58.

Sharran’s persistence rule reduces a large emotional commitment to a smaller daily decision: do not quit today, and reconsider tomorrow. He contrasts this with relying only on an inspiring reason for a long-term goal. When tired, discouraged, or under pressure, even a meaningful reason can become negotiable.

The rule changes the burden of continuing. A person does not have to settle every doubt about the eventual outcome before taking the next day’s action. Postponing the decision to quit preserves movement through a difficult period without requiring constant confidence or enthusiasm.

He introduces the idea through his account of food insecurity during college and returning years later as a commencement speaker. The distance between those moments supplies the motivational force of the story. It does not demonstrate that every project should continue indefinitely or that persistence guarantees a comparable outcome.

The narrow contribution of the technique is practical: it offers a repeatable response for a bad day. The question becomes whether to continue this day, rather than whether the entire future can be justified while exhausted. Within Sharran’s account, that smaller commitment helps protect meaningful work from a temporary emotional state.
