# Asset-backed borrowing

> You do not have to sell your assets to use their value.

[Buy, Borrow, Die: The Top 0.01% Strategy to Avoid Taxes](../sources/buy-borrow-die-the-top-0-01-strategy-to-avoid-taxes-9EF3R3JR5Wk.md), 0:03:04.

Asset-backed borrowing separates owning an investment from accessing some of its value. Rather than sell a holding, the owner pledges collateral and receives a loan. Sharran’s attraction to the structure is that the original asset can remain invested while the borrowed funds serve another purpose.

He describes three routes. A home-equity line uses property value as security. A securities-backed line uses eligible investments in a taxable brokerage account. A permanent-life-insurance loan draws on accumulated policy cash value. He emphasizes assets a lender can value and underwrite, alongside a track record of responsible borrowing. An asset being exciting or potentially profitable does not establish that a bank will accept it as collateral.

The tax argument is his claim that borrowing generally does not create income in the way selling appreciated assets can create a taxable gain. He also describes restrictions on particular accounts and uses of loan proceeds. These are simplified statements about the US setting, not a basis for assuming every account, transaction, or eventual distribution receives the same treatment.

The risks make clear why access to cash is not free wealth. Variable home-equity rates can increase payments, and default can threaten the home. A decline in securities collateral can provoke a demand for cash or forced sales. Unpaid policy loans and accumulating interest can exceed cash value, lapse insurance coverage, and create tax consequences in his account.

Sharran consequently stresses monitoring loan balances against the supporting asset values. His broader legacy-loop example also acknowledges that borrowing does not disappear at death: heirs must settle it. Keeping an investment intact changes the form and timing of funding, while preserving repayment obligations and exposing the pledged assets to the loan’s terms.
