# Customer Discovery

> it's really important to ask that person why they bought.

[Exactly How I Built a $3B+ Business in My 30s (3 Pillars)](../sources/exactly-how-i-built-a-3b-business-in-my-30s-3-pillars-7OSLclpwAkI.md), 0:03:26.

Srivatsaa describes customer discovery as asking existing buyers what they actually value before deciding how to describe the business. The founder’s explanation for starting a company may differ from the customer’s reason for joining, buying, or staying. That gap matters because a message built around the wrong benefit can overlook the strongest reason to choose the offer.

## Replace the founder’s guess with the customer’s reason

His central case concerns interviewing the twenty eight agents at a small real estate brokerage. He initially expected answers about the brand, leadership, culture, or competitive fees. In his account, those were secondary. The recurring primary answer was that the platform saved roughly one day of work per week.

He translated that answer into a promise about what an agent could do with another fifty two days each year. Discovery therefore changed the commercial message, rather than merely producing positive feedback about the existing company. The claimed business growth that followed is his account and does not independently isolate the interviews as its cause.

A second brokerage example identifies multiple ways of earning income as the important benefit. The difference reinforces that the answer is specific to what customers perceive in a given offer.

## Ask what would make staying impossible

His diagnostic question asks which single benefit, if removed, would cause the person to stop working with the business. It is designed to expose the essential reason beneath a longer list of desirable features. He acknowledges that sellers can develop an initial hypothesis, but argues they cannot reliably supply someone else’s reason without asking.

The resulting language also helps customers explain the business to others. A benefit they already experience is easier to repeat than an abstract claim supplied by marketing. Discovery thus connects the customer’s lived experience to a clearer value proposition and more coherent referrals.
