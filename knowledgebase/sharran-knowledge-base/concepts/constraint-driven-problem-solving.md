# Constraint Driven Problem Solving

> They changed the question itself.

[Starting From $1000 in 2026? Here’s Exactly How I’d Invest.](../sources/starting-from-1000-in-2026-here-s-exactly-how-i-d-invest-5A7uHZd1aGw.md), 0:02:47.

Srivatsaa uses constraints to interrupt the first obvious answer to a problem. Instead of asking only how to use a resource, he asks what the result would need to look like under a deliberate limit. A second version of the teaching examines whether the apparent limit is a real requirement or merely an assumption inherited from other people.

## Change the question before expanding the effort

His one hour question asks what business someone could build with $1,000 if it had to generate $500 a month while requiring no more than an hour of work a day. The limits force the idea to become specific before money is spent. The income target is a design constraint for an experiment, not an outcome the video proves anyone can achieve.

A classroom anecdote pushes the exercise further. Rather than spend the starting money on inventory, software, or leverage, one group allegedly sold advertising within its presentation to classmates. The presentation slot became the asset. The transcript gives inconsistent starting cash amounts, so the durable point is the reframing rather than exact financial reconstruction.

## Separate the objective from the assumed sequence

In another video, Srivatsaa uses a Dave Matthews story about reversing the expected order of lyrics and music. He generalizes it to beliefs about needing credentials, money, connections, or permission before beginning. Naming those assumptions makes them available for challenge; he also suggests asking AI to help question the frame.

The two approaches fit together. An intentionally imposed constraint can reveal an overlooked resource, while removing an unjustified assumed constraint can reveal an overlooked route. Neither requires rejecting every practical limitation. The decision is which part of the current question actually serves the objective and which part has been accepted without examination.
