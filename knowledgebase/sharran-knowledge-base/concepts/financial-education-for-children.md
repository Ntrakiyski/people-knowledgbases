# Financial education for children

> Teaching your children how to work is more valuable than any amount of money you could leave behind.

[Millionaire Advice I'm Giving My Kids I Wish I Knew in My 20s](../sources/millionaire-advice-i-m-giving-my-kids-i-wish-i-knew-in-my-20s-ISQkh8bu6EU.md), 0:00:49.

Sharran wants children to learn how value is created before they become responsible for a pool of family assets. His approach combines real work, visible payment, ownership decisions, and generosity. An inheritance structure alone does not provide the judgment needed to manage what it holds.

He distinguishes unpaid household responsibilities from useful paid work. Cleaning up is part of belonging to a family; earning should connect effort with a contribution someone values. He recommends praising effort and professional pride rather than relying on generic claims that a child is talented. Real customers and documented payments let the child see why money changes hands.

His daughter’s small e-commerce business supplies one example. The point was handling actual orders and responsibilities, not operating a pretend shop. His son’s ghostwriting supplies another: learn a skill, deliver for a client, receive recorded income, and understand the associated tax return. In a separate account, book reading earns money available for investing, creating an opening for family discussions about ownership and time.

Those conversations extend beyond what to buy. Sharran discusses volatility, thematic exposure, holding periods, and the patience to leave investments in place. He also involves children in selecting charities and explaining the intended effect of a donation. Stewardship therefore includes creating, managing, and giving value rather than merely protecting a balance.

His accounts emphasize different funding arrangements. The [investing-system video](../sources/when-i-understood-this-about-investing-my-life-changed-_Kpi8HgzKw8.md) says his children’s earnings go into Roth IRAs; the [millionaire-kids video](../sources/millionaire-advice-i-m-giving-my-kids-i-wish-i-knew-in-my-20s-ISQkh8bu6EU.md) describes intentionally designed whole-life policies instead of Roth IRA or 529 contributions. The relationship between these statements is left unexplained. Earned-income contributions and separately funded policies could coexist, but the transcript does not supply that reconciliation. The recurring educational principle is practical participation and open money conversations, without assuming how those separate funding accounts fit together.
