# Opportunistic Cash

> always having 20% of my portfolio in opportunistic cash.

[This Warren Buffett Rule Can Make You Rich (Copy THIS)](../sources/this-warren-buffett-rule-can-make-you-rich-copy-this-tzUvZijbpjY.md), 0:05:32.

Opportunistic cash is money assigned to readiness. Srivatsaa argues that an investor can recognize an attractive opportunity and still be unable to act because every available dollar is already committed elsewhere. A liquid reserve addresses that execution constraint rather than predicting when the next market dislocation will occur.

## Make an opportunity fund available in advance

He connects the practice to Buffett’s instruction about acting when others are fearful. In his account, periods of widespread fear can produce opportunities that do not remain available for long. Having to sell an existing position or find new capital only after noticing one can prevent acting on the thesis.

Srivatsaa says he keeps about twenty percent of his portfolio available for this purpose. One video suggests ten to twenty percent as a possible range; another states his rule as at least twenty percent. These are versions of his personal practice, not a demonstrated optimum for every portfolio. His wording also includes assets that can be liquidated readily, so the category is defined partly by accessibility rather than by a fully specified list of holdings.

His examples are autobiographical. He credits available cash with letting him enter the market during the early 2000s downturn and purchase selected stocks during the pandemic. The transcripts do not independently establish those transaction histories or show that every deployment of a reserve will succeed.

## Cash has a job, but the investment still needs a thesis

The reserve solves availability of capital. It does not by itself establish that a falling asset is undervalued or that a frightened market has reached a bottom. In the surrounding investing system, conviction, position limits, and periodic reviews remain separate tasks.

The useful distinction is therefore between intentionally reserved capacity and cash that has never been assigned a purpose. Srivatsaa’s opportunity reserve is intended to make a previously considered investment possible when circumstances change.
