# Cash-flow investing

> is it something that pays you to own it?

[This Goldman Sachs Investing Formula Makes Millionaires](../sources/this-goldman-sachs-investing-formula-makes-millionaires-rtJt16ty4xI.md), 0:04:46.

Sharran separates the money an investment pays while it is held from the change in what it could be sold for. Both can contribute to an investment outcome, but they answer different questions. A growing sale value does not necessarily supply current spending money.

This is why cash flow receives its own category in his investment x-ray. Alongside capital preservation, tax efficiency, and growth, he asks whether an asset pays the owner. Examples include Treasury interest, dividends, and property distributions. His preferred working definition of an asset emphasizes that payment function.

The Treasury example makes the distinction concrete. He describes receiving a yield and eventually getting the stated principal back, rather than expecting the principal itself to appreciate. That is his simplified holding-to-maturity illustration, not a complete explanation of a bond's changing market price.

His discussion of dividend funds asks whether a broad equity investment can add distributions while retaining some growth exposure. He names his own SCHD holding as an example. This is a personal selection within the framework rather than evidence that adding dividends automatically improves every portfolio.

Life stage changes the weight of the cash-flow question. He says his young daughter does not need investments to fund current purchases because her family supplies them. A much older investor may have a different need. Cash flow is therefore a dimension to assess, not an instruction to maximize payouts regardless of purpose.

The distinction also clarifies his financial-freedom goal. Income from owned assets can increasingly cover expenses without requiring another sale or another hour of work. Evaluating that mechanism separately makes it possible to ask what a holding contributes today and what depends on future appreciation.
