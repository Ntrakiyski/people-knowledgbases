# Pairs Trading

> Think about a pair trade.

[After 31 Years of Investing, THIS Is My Exact System (Copy Me)](../sources/after-31-years-of-investing-this-is-my-exact-system-copy-me-e7jNOBjblwk.md), 0:04:32.

Sharran introduces pair trading as part of a two-job rule for investments. Rather than holding an isolated belief that one company will do well, he wants to express a comparison between two businesses or between a business and a wider market.

His examples include Coke versus Pepsi and Home Depot versus Lowe's. A relative thesis asks which business should outperform and why. That is more specific than merely deciding that a familiar company is attractive. It focuses attention on differences within a comparison rather than treating every broad market headline as equally important.

He says pairing can cancel market noise and describes believing one company will rise while another falls. However, the video does not spell out how both sides are implemented. It does not establish whether the second leg is a short position, how the legs are sized, what financing costs apply, or how remaining market exposure is measured.

Those omissions matter because comparing two companies is not itself proof of a hedge. Buying both, selling one, and shorting one are different transactions with different consequences. The page preserves his relative-thesis idea without converting his broad market-neutrality language into a completed trading strategy.

He also mentions tax-loss harvesting as another reason to think about substitutes, using a sale after a loss and a different purchase as an illustration. That is related portfolio activity, not the same mechanism as a fully specified relative-value trade.

The supported lesson is narrower than a ready-to-execute system: make the comparative belief explicit, examine how the two ideas interact, and recognize that the source leaves important execution and risk mechanics unresolved.
