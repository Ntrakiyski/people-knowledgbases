# Asset Allocation

> no position is greater than 5%.

[After 31 Years of Investing, THIS Is My Exact System (Copy Me)](../sources/after-31-years-of-investing-this-is-my-exact-system-copy-me-e7jNOBjblwk.md), 0:02:56.

Sharran treats allocation primarily as a survival question: how much of the portfolio can depend on one position or one shared idea? An investment can sound attractive while still being too large relative to everything the investor has built.

His cautionary account concerns a friend putting roughly $700,000, described as fifteen years of savings, into one oil-and-gas opportunity. The deal failed. Sharran emphasizes the size of the bet rather than making the entire lesson about the promoter's friendship or the apparent attractiveness of the offer. A single failure should not destroy the base from which future opportunities can be pursued.

In his personal-system video, he states two guardrails: no individual position above 5%, and no theme above 10%. Tesla illustrates a position; technology illustrates a theme. The second limit addresses the possibility that apparently separate holdings depend on the same broad thesis. He presents these rules as a way to diversify when he is not watching markets continuously.

The source does not provide a fully consistent allocation recipe. A 5% maximum does not imply a maximum of twenty positions. Later references to seven to ten holdings, the position limits, and his cash rule are not fully reconciled. These statements should remain visible as his separate guardrails rather than being combined into an invented model portfolio.

He connects sizing to seeking similar returns with less risk. The objective is not merely the highest upside forecast, but preserving the capacity to remain invested and act again. The percentages are his stated approach; the more general lesson is to size an idea in relation to the damage its failure could cause.
