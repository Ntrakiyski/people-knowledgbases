# Sales Funnels

> you can use a small brand to build a big business if your funnel is really simple

[Exactly How I Built a $3B+ Business in My 30s (3 Pillars)](../sources/exactly-how-i-built-a-3b-business-in-my-30s-3-pillars-7OSLclpwAkI.md), 0:16:37.

A sales funnel connects a defined audience with a repeatable path to a customer conversation. Sharran’s emphasis is on a simple sequence that a team can perform consistently, rather than a large following or a different campaign every day.

His Real brokerage example starts with Instagram posts promoting speaking appearances. People attend a relevant local presentation, then enter chat and direct-message follow-up. A team continues those conversations and brings interested agents into the business. The stages connect the content audience, the event, and a specific next interaction instead of leaving attention disconnected from recruitment.

He reports substantial agent growth from this engine while describing his following as a relatively small niche brand. Those growth and attribution figures are his account, not independently established evidence that the funnel alone produced the outcome. The conceptual claim is that relevance within a narrow market can matter more than broad public fame.

A brokerage podcast offers a second route. Material originally intended for internal use became public, allowing outsiders to hear the company’s real discussions and lessons. Sharran says that authenticity made more people want to join. Here, repeated communication gives prospective recruits a view of the organization before a direct conversation.

The common mechanism is continuity. A clear value proposition attracts the right people, a familiar process supplies the next step, and repeated execution makes the system easier to operate. Attention becomes commercially useful through that connected path, rather than being treated as an outcome by itself.
