# Leadership standards

> A great leader's job is to define what good looks like.

[If You Only Watch One Business Video, Make It This](../sources/if-you-only-watch-one-business-video-make-it-this-wXhUtIvnr9U.md), 0:24:49.

Srivatsaa defines a leader’s responsibility as making good work visible. An employee can have the necessary skills and motivation while still missing an outcome that exists only in the leader’s head. Before treating that gap as a failure of effort or talent, the leader should ask whether the expected standard was ever communicated clearly.

## Show the work someone should be able to model

His example concerns an employee writing memos in a style the leader dislikes. Instead of beginning with a reprimand, the leader acknowledges the effort and accepts responsibility for unclear guidance. The next step is concrete: provide three memos that represent the organization’s gold standard and ask the employee to model future work on them.

This changes the problem from guessing an unstated preference to comparing an output with visible examples. Both employee and manager can identify the remaining difference. The examples communicate quality, not merely the existence of a task on a checklist.

## Standards support feedback and assessment

Srivatsaa imagines extending this clarity across the tasks of an organization. When good work has been defined, a new employee can see what is expected and a manager can judge performance against a common reference. Responsibility for making that reference available remains with leadership.

He applies the principle to virtual assistants and AI instructions as well: a sample slide deck or demonstrated approach can be more useful than a vague request to produce something good. His broader comments about AI are illustrative rather than a technical account of model behavior.

The video does not reject procedures in every setting. He explicitly allows detailed instructions for highly technical work. The distinction is between useful specificity and assuming a long procedure automatically conveys the desired quality. An example and an explicit outcome often expose the target more directly.
