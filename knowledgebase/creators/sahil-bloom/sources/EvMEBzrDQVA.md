# Stop Setting Goals. Do This Instead.

This page describes the selected source's claims and advice. The summary and interpretations are machine-extracted drafts; quotations have been matched against the exported transcript.

Review scope: Codex editorial draft; complete local transcript read; claims not independently verified.

Method: Direct Codex extraction from complete local transcript; exact quotations verified locally.

Bloom's framework keeps traditional goals: choose an arena, set the desired result, imagine both failure and success that damages life, then define anti-goals to avoid those outcomes. Worked examples cover weight, family and friends, dating, and money. The rules protect sustainable routines, meaningful presence, values, flexibility, and health. A closing section addresses chaos with a two-day rule, tiny beneficial action, and A/B/C versions of daily effort. Despite the title, he does not tell viewers to abandon goals. The distinctive idea is to define acceptable means and protected priorities alongside a target, then build a flexible process.

## Context and limits

The ninety-two-percent failure claim and scientific certainty about missing two days are not verified. Boundaries phrased as 'never' may need revision under real obligations and changing circumstances. Health and financial examples are general illustrations, not personalized plans. The framework cannot guarantee happiness or intact relationships.
