Bloom argues that following borrowed desires can produce a life that looks attractive but feels empty. He recommends identifying energizing interests, acting consistently despite imperfection, treating some discomfort as growth, maintaining high internal standards without demanding external applause, and taking responsibility. The section on low expectations concerns uncontrollable recognition, not careless work: he explicitly retains preparation and mastery. Self-designed rewards are offered as a way to sustain effort. Claims equating discomfort with progress or personal responsibility with total control require qualification.
