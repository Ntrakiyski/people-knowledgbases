Bloom separates management, creation, consumption, and ideation time. He argues that fast administrative completions can feel productive while displacing work with slower payoffs. His proposed audit colors a recent calendar in half-hour increments, then batches administration and reserves daily windows for creation, learning, and thinking. He treats reading and quiet reflection as inputs to creative work rather than inherently wasted time. Neuroscience explanations and a walking-study statistic are invoked without sufficient detail to assess them. The protocol is a scheduling hypothesis, not proof that management work is unimportant or that the suggested allocation fits every role.
