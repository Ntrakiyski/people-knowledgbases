Asked whether a service-business mindset applies to creators and people who do not want large teams, the respondent argues that distant plans and celebrity-founder examples can make ordinary opportunities feel too small. He broadens the intended audience to employees and families as well as future owners. The discussion then turns to skills he considers underpracticed in conventional education: making decisions, delegating, and persuading others. Exaggerated classroom and sports examples illustrate the point rather than prescribe cheating or avoiding exercise. The message favors learning from practical responsibility, but does not demonstrate that formal education or studying major founders is generally harmful.
