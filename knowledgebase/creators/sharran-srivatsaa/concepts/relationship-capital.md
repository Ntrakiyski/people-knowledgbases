# Relationship Capital

> What 10 relationships can I invest in wholeheartedly for the next 10 years

[I'm 46. If You Want to Get Rich With Investing, Watch This:](../sources/i-m-46-if-you-want-to-get-rich-with-investing-watch-this-tT_Gp5e8vbo.md), 0:14:46.

Sharran treats trusted relationships as an asset built through sustained support, attention, and demonstrated usefulness. His central example is the 10–10–forever rule: identify a small group of people to support wholeheartedly over the next ten years, with the intention of relationships that last beyond that period.

Ten is a concentration limit rather than a quota to fill immediately. He says his own list began with one person and, in another account, still contained only seven names after many years. The work includes making introductions, buying products, promoting projects, answering calls, and recognizing important personal events. The question is how to help the other person become successful.

He explicitly rejects a short-term scorecard. An introduction does not create an immediate obligation to return a favor of equal value. His claim that a handful of relationships created most of his wealth explains his enthusiasm, but it does not establish a predictable return for every act of generosity.

His banking story adds another mechanism: experienced people may advocate for someone who shows coachability. Asking for a prospecting script demonstrated willingness to learn, and he says the managing partner subsequently supported him in hiring and internal conversations. Trust can influence decisions made when the person benefiting is not in the room.

Attention is part of the investment. His instruction to be where one's feet are means showing up fully for the interaction at hand, while limiting energy spent on hostile strangers outside those priorities.

The Pokémon rescue narrative supplies the crisis version of the same lesson: relationships established before urgent need can make cooperation possible when time and money run short. The recurring principle is to build trust before depending on it.
