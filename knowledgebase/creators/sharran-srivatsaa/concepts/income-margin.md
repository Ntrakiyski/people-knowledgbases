# Income Margin

> Margin is the difference between what you earn and what you spend.

[Once I Hit These Money Milestones, I Became Financially Free](../sources/once-i-hit-these-money-milestones-i-became-financially-free-aetr9LovwE4.md), 0:00:30.

Income margin is the part of earnings that remains available after the current lifestyle is funded. Sharran places it before complex investment strategies because assets, debt reduction, and additional training all require resources that current bills have not already consumed. A prestigious salary does not establish that this surplus exists.

There are two connected levers: increase earning capacity and stop expenses from automatically following it upward. His examples contrast someone who retains part of a modest income with someone spending every dollar of a much larger one. In another illustration, a $500,000 earner investing five percent contributes less than a $150,000 earner investing thirty-five percent. These comparisons concern contributions, not proof of any particular investment outcome.

He also calls the margin a wealth buffer. Expressed as a percentage, it is income minus spending, divided by income. This makes changes in lifestyle visible even when the paycheck grows. New housing, car payments, and other recurring obligations can absorb a raise before the extra money ever reaches investments.

His household example is a long spending freeze: he says his wife’s income funded their baseline lifestyle while his business income went toward assets, and that they retained the separation after earnings improved. The transferable mechanism is a predetermined lifestyle allowance rather than repeatedly deciding how much of each windfall to consume.

Margin is therefore both a stability measure and the funding source for later milestones. Where expenses exceed income, Sharran emphasizes addressing that mismatch first, including through targeted skill development. Where positive margin already exists, protecting it from lifestyle inflation lets increased earnings improve the balance sheet instead of merely raising the cost of remaining employed.
