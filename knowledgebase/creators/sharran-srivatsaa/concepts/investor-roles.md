# Investor Roles

> active investors, passive investors, and thematic

[After 31 Years of Investing, THIS Is My Exact System (Copy Me)](../sources/after-31-years-of-investing-this-is-my-exact-system-copy-me-e7jNOBjblwk.md), 0:08:24.

Srivatsaa asks investors to identify the work they actually intend to do before selecting assets or tactics. His three roles are active, thematic, and passive. The distinction concerns responsibility and attention rather than whether someone owns a particular ticker or uses a fashionable investment label.

## Who makes the investment work?

An active investor researches, finds, trades, manages, or operates investments as a substantial job. Srivatsaa’s examples include a professional stock trader and a person whose work is investing in real estate. He warns that copying their tactics while occupied by a separate career can create stress without the necessary attention or process.

A thematic investor expresses a longer term belief about an area such as healthcare, AI, technology, a country, or gold. A sector fund can provide exposure without requiring the investor to operate every underlying business. The work shifts toward choosing and maintaining a coherent thesis. The existence of a plausible theme does not itself establish that its current price is attractive.

A passive investor, in his terminology, selects an active manager or operator who performs the underlying work. An apartment investment may be passive for the capital provider while someone else handles the property. Fees compensate that work in his account. He repeatedly asks who is responsible when a chain of participants all describe their role as passive.

## Keep the definition attached to the speaker

This taxonomy is Srivatsaa’s teaching framework. His statement that passive investing requires an active manager is narrower than common uses of the term and should not be turned into a universal definition of index investing.

The practical purpose is to align strategy with available expertise, time, and accountability. Choosing a role helps explain what must be monitored, what is being delegated, and why an investment should fit the rest of the investor’s life.
