# Intentional Resource Allocation

> Investing is the intentional allocation of resources to hit a specific goal.

[I'm 46. If You Want to Get Rich With Investing, Watch This:](../sources/i-m-46-if-you-want-to-get-rich-with-investing-watch-this-tT_Gp5e8vbo.md), 0:00:36.

Srivatsaa broadens investing from purchasing securities to deliberately allocating resources toward a chosen outcome. His five forms of capital are money, skill, time, relationships, and brand. The model asks what someone already has available and how its use connects to the life or opportunity they want to create.

## An allocation happens even without a plan

In this framing, habits are already investing resources. Time is spent, relationships receive or lose attention, and behavior shapes reputation whether or not a formal decision has been made. Srivatsaa’s agency argument is that making these allocations visible creates a chance to change the next one.

He describes imagining three life paths: continue the current trajectory, choose an alternative if the present profession vanished, and imagine a path unconstrained by money or status. The purpose is to reveal that a current path is a choice rather than an inevitable destination. It is an exercise in direction setting before selecting tactics.

## Resources can complement each other

Someone without much cash may contribute time or a useful capability. Someone with an established audience may contribute association or introductions. A person with money may fund work that another person can execute. Srivatsaa repeatedly asks what can be traded to obtain the desired result, rather than assuming cash is the only acceptable contribution.

Each resource also has its own maintenance mechanism. Money can be placed in compounding vehicles; skill improves through practice; time benefits from conscious boundaries; relationships require support and attention; reputation develops through behavior and proof. Treating them all as capital does not make them interchangeable in every circumstance.

The model is an inventory for deliberate choice, not a promise that every friendship, hour, or skill will generate a financial return. Its common standard is a connection between the resource committed and the outcome intended. That makes a course, conversation, practice session, or portfolio decision comparable as an allocation without pretending they have identical payoffs.
