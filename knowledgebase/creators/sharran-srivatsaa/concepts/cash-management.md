# Cash Management

> That's exactly my portfolio and it's divided equally.

[Passive Income Is Simple, Once You Have a Plan Like THIS:](../sources/passive-income-is-simple-once-you-have-a-plan-like-this-qi4gqXD4t-g.md), 0:07:47.

Sharran’s cash-management question is what a dollar must do while it remains available for future use. He contrasts money idle in a low-interest checking account with arrangements intended to earn income, preserve access, or provide other exposures. The choice is therefore about both a job for the funds and the conditions under which they may be needed.

He discusses high-yield savings and certificates of deposit alongside a personal four-fund portfolio. His stated fund allocation is equal portions of SCHO, SCHD, TIP, and SGOV. Elsewhere he describes the mix as combining Treasury exposure, inflation linkage, distributions, and some equity growth. This is his attributed example; the videos do not establish that its components behave like insured bank cash or that a quoted yield makes it appropriate for every reserve.

A CD ladder addresses a more specific access problem. Instead of committing everything until one distant date, his example divides the balance into three-, six-, nine-, twelve-, and fifteen-month maturities. A portion then matures every three months. The mechanism is scheduled access to a tranche of capital rather than a single all-or-nothing maturity date.

His description of CDs as inaccessible during a term simplifies the product discussion. The central teaching does not require that absolute claim: staggering dates changes when funds become available under the chosen schedule. Similarly, a portfolio intended to distribute income is a different arrangement from money whose immediate nominal value must remain stable.

Across the examples, Sharran presents his own holdings transparently and encourages assessing suitability with an adviser. The source material supports comparing access needs with instrument characteristics. It does not supply a complete analysis of each fund’s price behavior, tax effects, or risk, so personal portfolio examples remain distinct from a universally safe cash allocation.
