# Wealth Ladder

> you need to do is to have a decent life is to have your active income greater than your monthly expenses.

[If You Only Watch One Business Video, Make It This](../sources/if-you-only-watch-one-business-video-make-it-this-wXhUtIvnr9U.md), 0:18:10.

The wealth ladder connects four activities that Srivatsaa argues are often taught separately: earning income, retaining a surplus, owning productive assets, and receiving income from those assets. A better paid job can improve the first stage without completing any of the later ones. The framework is meant to identify the next missing mechanism rather than label every high earner financially independent.

## From earnings to ownership

The first diagnostic question is whether active income covers monthly expenses. Active income still depends on work, but it supplies the starting fuel. The next question is whether some of that fuel remains available for another purpose. Without a spending boundary or deliberate allocation, a raise can simply finance a more expensive lifestyle.

Surplus then needs a route into assets. Srivatsaa calls this infrastructure a money factory in another version of the teaching: active earnings enter one zone, lifestyle spending has a defined role, and invested surplus feeds a separate passive income zone. His family example uses one spouse’s income for household costs while directing the other’s earnings toward the future income system.

The ownership step can begin with tiny income generating assets rather than a whole company or building. Its purpose is to learn to allocate and own, not merely to accumulate a larger bank balance.

## The final rung changes the source of coverage

At the last stage, asset income rather than additional work covers living expenses. The relevant comparison is income received against the lifestyle being funded. A large salary alone says little about this relationship if no assets or allocation habits follow it.

Across the versions, the causal sequence is consistent even when the labels vary: marketable skill supports earnings, controlled spending creates investable capacity, deliberate investing builds ownership, and productive ownership can fund choices. The ladder does not supply a guaranteed timetable or a universally sufficient asset balance. It explains why earning skill and investing skill serve different jobs.
