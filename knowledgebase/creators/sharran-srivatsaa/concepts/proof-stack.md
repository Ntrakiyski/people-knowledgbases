# Proof Stack

> What is more important is proof over promise.

[I'm 46. If You Want to Get Rich With Investing, Watch This:](../sources/i-m-46-if-you-want-to-get-rich-with-investing-watch-this-tT_Gp5e8vbo.md), 0:19:55.

Sharran uses proof to reduce how much faith another person must place in a promise. Competence, commitment, and care become easier to evaluate when expressed through visible work rather than asserted in a pitch.

His Pokémon analysis organizes the proof stack into demonstrated delivery, relationships, and commitment. Prior shipped work answers whether someone can deliver. Established relationships answer whether that person can be trusted when conditions become difficult. Visible sacrifice and consistency address whether the person will keep working after receiving help. These are the elements he draws from his account of the deal, rather than a guarantee that every similarly prepared project succeeds.

Concrete artifacts make the idea useful outside fundraising. Founders can show prototypes, actual users, revenue, or case studies. Job seekers can solve a relevant employer problem instead of relying only on a résumé. A professional can teach or demonstrate an outcome. His grill analogy contrasts saying that a product works with showing it cook.

Preparation is another form of proof. He recommends selecting an upcoming meeting and arriving with unusually thorough research, notes, and analysis. His account of the Hormozis rapidly completing a framework illustrates follow-through as evidence of serious engagement. Much of the apparent ease of the final presentation comes from work completed beforehand.

A written plan can also expose thinking. His example of reviewing and revising a child's proposal makes both the intended action and the learning process inspectable.

Across these examples, the mechanism is the same: locate the uncertainty between a decision maker and agreement, then show relevant evidence that shrinks it. Building that evidence before urgently needing a job, investor, or rescue makes it more persuasive than a last-minute declaration of enthusiasm.
