Sharran’s review cadence gives different decisions a recurring place in the calendar. It combines awareness of current holdings with scheduled strategy maintenance and a less frequent decision about positions that may no longer belong. He presents this as a personal system for feeling connected to investments without continually chasing new recommendations.

The weekly review is a five-to-ten-minute account check, which he adds to his Friday review-and-preview routine. He looks at positions, notices whether values have moved, and checks for anything unexpected. Its purpose is orientation rather than automatically trading in response to every change.

The monthly review handles his pair trades and options. He checks whether relative-performance positions are working and whether options need closing within the thirty-to-forty-five-day cycles he describes. Viewers without those strategies may instead review thematic holdings. His claimed time requirement is personal; the transcript does not establish that this schedule adequately monitors every derivative or trading risk.

Quarterly, he spends time on declining holdings and tax-loss harvesting. The central question is whether he still wants to own a position. If yes, he leaves it; if no, he sells, records the loss, and moves into a different investment or opportunistic cash. A lower price prompts a deliberate question rather than an automatic exit.

He connects the routine with a manageable number of holdings and a clear investor role. The benefit he claims is confidence in a repeatable process, rather than confidence that every investment will rise. Scheduled attention supports a long-term system, but the video does not prove that the same intervals suit assets with different liquidity, leverage, or monitoring requirements.
