Sharran’s speed formula links opportunity, plan, action, and time. Its target is the delay between recognizing a useful opening and executing a workable response. Speed in this framework includes preparation and adaptation; it is not simply acting without a structure.

Opportunity begins with a market gap. A new technology, an unsolved business problem, or an unusual combination of skills can create an opening. Seeing it is insufficient if the person lacks the capabilities to act. Elsewhere in the same explanation, Sharran emphasizes developing relevant skills before the opportunity arrives.

Plan asks what must be true for the response to work and how the steps fit together. In his OpenAI deal narrative, he describes a structure combining recruitment of a creator, continued independence for an open-source project, and acceptable compensation. These are his reconstruction and interpretation of the reported deal, rather than an independently verified account of its terms.

Action then puts the plan into motion while incorporating new information. Sharran attributes success in the case to willingness to adjust terms as the counterparty’s priorities became clearer. A rigid plan executed slowly can miss an opening even if its original analysis seemed thorough.

Time connects the other elements: how long does it take to move from recognition to structure to action? He argues that a workable plan pursued promptly can outperform prolonged perfectionism. His claims that the formula creates millionaires are promotional. The supported operating lesson is to identify where recognition, planning, and execution are becoming disconnected, then reduce unnecessary delay while preserving the flexibility to learn.
