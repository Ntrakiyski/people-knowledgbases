Sharran puts strategy before listings. The buyer first explains the life event motivating the move, identifies unacceptable features and genuine must-haves, separates optional conveniences, and distinguishes hard deadlines from flexible ones. Financing belongs in this initial brief: a cash purchase and a mortgage-backed purchase create different liquidity and execution considerations.

His seven-out-of-ten rule makes tradeoffs explicit. Choose between a stronger house in a weaker location and a weaker house in a stronger location, rather than expecting both to be perfect. The scores are a decision aid drawn from his claimed transaction experience. Their purpose is to keep negotiable property features from becoming confused with priorities that are harder to change.

The agent then helps execute the plan. Sharran recommends interviewing candidates about their buying process, current avoidable mistakes, and compensation. Mortgage buyers should obtain pre-approval before searching seriously and avoid changing the financial profile through new debt, credit accounts, or employment changes during the process.

Search extends beyond active listings. He describes conditionally available owners who might sell if their price, replacement-home, or financing needs were met. He also uses months of inventory to interpret negotiating conditions, with five to six months as his balanced-market benchmark. Asking whether the seller is pricing aspirationally, near comparables, or below them to create an event helps the buyer interpret the asking price.

Once an offer is accepted, the work changes from selection to verification. Inspection, loan, and appraisal contingencies can provide routes to investigate, renegotiate, or leave within the applicable periods. These are contractual opportunities, not universal rights independent of the agreement. Closing adds moving logistics, utilities, a final walkthrough, document signing, and funds transfer. Across the four stages, the buyer supplies a clear target while the agent manages the transaction process and its market-specific details.
