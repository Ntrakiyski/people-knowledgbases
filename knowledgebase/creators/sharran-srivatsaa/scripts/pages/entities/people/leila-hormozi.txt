Sharran credits Leila Hormozi with the leadership principle of defining what good looks like. He develops the idea through an employee-memo example: a leader who wants a different result should provide a clear model and explain the expected standard.

Her role in the corpus is as the attributed source of that principle. The resulting lesson places responsibility on the manager to make quality observable before interpreting a disappointing result as an employee’s failure. It connects leadership standards with communication and teaching. The transcript renders her name inconsistently; the canonical name is used here while supporting quotations retain their original wording.
