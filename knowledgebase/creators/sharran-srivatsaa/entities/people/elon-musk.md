> Stanford will always be there. The startup won't go build the startup.

[Being Around The Top 0.01% Teaches You This](../../sources/being-around-the-top-0-01-teaches-you-this-BZrgkgCF79g.md), 0:06:14.

Elon Musk appears in Sharran’s account of choosing between study at Stanford and pursuing a startup. Sharran attributes advice to Musk that the school opportunity would remain available while the venture’s timing might not.

He uses the story to explain decision reversibility alongside opportunity timing. Some choices permit a later return; others close an opening while a person deliberates. The anecdote supports Sharran’s decision framework rather than an instruction to leave education or prioritize every startup. The conversation and its effect are reported from his personal recollection, without independent verification in the corpus.
