# This Warren Buffett Rule Can Make You Rich (Copy THIS)

Srivatsaa describes three investing lessons he attributes to Warren Buffett: make large but considered bets, retain dry powder, and imagine a lifetime punch card with only ten investment decisions. He recounts Buffett investing in Goldman Sachs during the financial crisis and Berkshire investing in homebuilders, using those accounts to argue that confidence and liquidity permit buying when other investors are fearful. Several historical amounts and comparisons are approximate claims in his narration rather than verified transaction records.

He translates dry powder into keeping roughly 10% to 20% of a portfolio readily available for opportunities, citing his own pandemic stock purchases. The punch card idea is intended to counter fear of missing out and indiscriminate investing. To evaluate an opportunity, he briefly supplies his four part investment x-ray: capital preservation, tax efficiency, cash flow, and growth. His advocacy of concentrated conviction should be understood as his investment philosophy; describing a bet as safe does not establish that it is low risk.

## Concepts covered

- [Conviction and Investment Rationale](../concepts/conviction-investing.md): Know what you own, why you own it, and which evidence would change the decision.
- [Investment X-Ray](../concepts/investment-x-ray.md): Evaluate capital preservation, tax efficiency, cash flow, and growth before committing money.
- [Opportunistic Cash](../concepts/opportunistic-cash.md): Accessible reserves allow an investor to fund opportunities without waiting to free capital.
