# This Goldman Sachs Investing Formula Makes Millionaires

Sharran introduces an investment x-ray learned from a client: evaluate capital preservation, tax efficiency, yield or cash flow, and growth. He gives each category a subjective twenty-five-point allocation so that investments can be compared on a hundred-point scale. Examples include gold, a friend’s startup, broad-market and dividend ETFs, Bitcoin, and real estate. He explicitly treats the scores as quick judgments rather than definitive answers and notes uncertainty about several product details.

He uses the framework to ask how an existing investment might activate another benefit, such as adding dividend income or improving property-management practicality. He also separates supporting friends’ businesses from investing in something one does not understand. The discussion includes his personal Bitcoin loss-harvesting practice and his claimed move from single-family homes to multifamily investments. Tax statements, classifications, expected doubling periods, and advantages of particular investments are his assertions or projections and require context; the durable teaching is to examine multiple dimensions before committing capital.

## Concepts covered

- [Cash-flow investing](../concepts/cash-flow-investing.md): Assess whether an investment pays income while it is held, separately from appreciation.
- [Conviction and Investment Rationale](../concepts/conviction-investing.md): Know what you own, why you own it, and which evidence would change the decision.
- [Investment X-Ray](../concepts/investment-x-ray.md): Evaluate capital preservation, tax efficiency, cash flow, and growth before committing money.
- [Risk Management](../concepts/risk-management.md): Evaluate downside, obligations, behavioral errors, and execution dependence before and after committing resources.
- [Tax Efficiency](../concepts/tax-efficiency.md): Assess how account placement, timing, and applicable rules affect after-tax investment outcomes.
