# Lionel Messi’s $1.5B Deal Can Make You Rich (Here’s How)

Sharran recounts Lionel Messi’s move to Inter Miami as a choice between guaranteed compensation and ownership upside. He describes MLS, Apple, Adidas, and the club as parties whose commercial interests depended on attracting global attention, and portrays Jorge Mas as preparing years ahead by changing ownership arrangements and organizing the conditions for Messi’s arrival. The contract figures, valuation changes, subscription growth, and future ownership percentage are the speaker’s reported claims, with the equity percentage explicitly described as rumored.

His analysis focuses on terms rather than headline price: salary supplies current income, revenue sharing ties compensation to subscriptions and merchandise, and equity participates in longer-term business value. He then maps debt, mezzanine finance, and ownership into a capital stack and explains the dependency risk of the whole arrangement. For viewers, he recommends building valuable skills, negotiating performance-linked compensation, and considering ownership in value they help create; his promise of becoming rich is promotional rather than an assured outcome.

## Concepts covered

- [Capital stack](../concepts/capital-stack.md): The capital stack maps the financing and ownership contributions supporting a deal.
- [Deal structure](../concepts/deal-structure.md): A transaction’s terms distribute control, ownership, obligations, and future upside.
- [Equity Ownership](../concepts/equity-ownership.md): Participate in business value through ownership, with the corresponding capital and execution risks.
- [Performance-based compensation](../concepts/performance-based-compensation.md): Compensation linked to measurable commercial results rather than only fixed salary.
- [Risk Management](../concepts/risk-management.md): Evaluate downside, obligations, behavioral errors, and execution dependence before and after committing resources.
